D5 Which data biases might lead to discrimination or otherwise impact fundamental rights?
    • D5.1
        ◦ Which attributes are salient or at risk of discrimination
        ◦ Example: 'sex' and 'ethnicity' are relevant because both correlate with care-seeking behaviour and diagnostic patterns
        ◦ Origin: Venus, 5; datasheet, 17
        ◦ Covers: Art. 10(2)(f), Art. 10(3)
    • D5.2
        ◦ Marginal distribution of protected attributes
        ◦ Example: 58% male, 42% female; 74% white, 12% South Asian, 14% other
        ◦ Origin: Venus, 5
        ◦ Covers: Art. 10(2)(f), Art. 10(3)
    • D5.3
        ◦ Joint distribution of protected and target
        ◦ Example: 30-day readmission rate is 18% for men and 11% for women
        ◦ Origin: Venus, 5
        ◦ Covers: Art. 10(2)(f), Art. 10(3)
    • D5.4
        ◦ Expected distributions
        ◦ Example: lower prevalence of sickle cell disease in white patients is expected and reflects epidemiology, not a data collection artefact
        ◦ Origin: Venus, 5
        ◦ Covers: Art. 10(2)(f), Art. 10(3)
    • D5.5
        ◦ Label provenance
        ◦ Example: 'ethnicity' was self-reported at registration; 'sex' was recorded by administrative staff and may conflate biological sex with gender
        ◦ Origin: Venus, 5
        ◦ Covers: Art. 10(2)(f), Art. 10(3)
    • D5.6
        ◦ Data-side bias mitigation applied
        ◦ Example: the South Asian subgroup was oversampled in the final dataset to correct for underrepresentation identified in D5.2; targeted re-annotation was performed for cases where inter-annotator agreement was below 0.6
        ◦ Origin: new
        ◦ Covers: Art. 10(2)(g) — data-level measures to detect, prevent and mitigate bias
